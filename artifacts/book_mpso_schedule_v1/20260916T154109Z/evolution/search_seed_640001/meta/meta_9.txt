# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best remains Change-Triggered Full Temporary Quantum Conversion:** `return 5 if observation["change_detected"] else 0`. Its score **0.3644**, mean error **1.7446**, worst-case error **2.2709**, and case-error SD **0.4853** remain strongest across all evaluated programs. The successful observed pattern is full conversion on detection followed immediately by ordinary neutral-particle updates.
- **Full conversion outperformed both reduced detection bursts.** Three Particle Quantum Conversion scored **0.3426**, with mean error **1.9191**; the new Four-Particle Quantum Burst scored **0.3198**, with mean error **2.1267**. Five conversions performed best, but four performed worse than three, so these results do not establish a monotonic relationship between conversion count and performance.
- **Conditional Follow-Up remains the strongest modification**, scoring **0.3554** versus **0.3445** for Three-Update Taper. Its shorter, improvement-gated continuation reduced mean error by **0.0888**, but still exceeded the best by **0.0692**. Duration and gating changed together, leaving their separate contributions unresolved.
## Ineffective Approaches
- **Even a light unconditional tail worsened performance.** Brief Quantum Conversion After Detected Changes uses a **5–1–1** schedule and scored **0.3289**, with mean error **2.0407**—**17.0% above the best**. Its conversion exposure was only **1.41–1.82%**, yet it performed worse than the earlier, heavier Three-Update Taper. Shorter or lighter tails therefore did not consistently improve results.
- **The 1% fitness threshold added no aggregate benefit.** Fitness-Threshold Temporary Quantum Conversion and Change-Triggered Quantum Conversion with a 1% Threshold both scored **0.3233**, with mean error **2.0930**, **20.0% above the best**. Their four-particle branch activated on only **3.3%** of detected-change updates; this infrequent departure from full conversion nevertheless coincided with substantial trajectory-level regressions.
- **Distributing exploration across updates remained inferior to concentrating it at detection.** The earlier **3–1–1 Schedule** scored **0.3366**, with mean error **1.9705**, versus **0.3644/1.7446** for the best. Five requested conversions across an uninterrupted sequence did not reproduce five conversions in one detection update. The new **5–1–1** result reinforces the observed disadvantage of unconditional continuation, without isolating its cause.
## Implementation Insights
- **The best rule tightly limits replacement of ordinary PSO motion.** Temporary conversions occurred exclusively on detection updates, covering **1.10–1.39%** of neutral-particle update opportunities. Returning zero subsequently preserves ordinary neutral-particle motion while the permanent quantum particle continues sampling. Thus, the best schedule retains ongoing exploration without a temporary-conversion tail.
- **Temporary quantum sampling replaces an ordinary update.** The tails alter particle trajectories rather than adding samples alongside unchanged PSO motion. Disrupted convergence is a plausible explanation for their regressions, but asynchronous attractor updates and subsequent random movements also diverge; the evaluations measure complete optimizer behavior, not an isolated causal effect.
- **Generations 6 and 8 are behaviorally equivalent.** `small_change_burst` nests the threshold inside the detection branch; `small_change_reserve` returns early without detection. Their saved case results are identical, so they represent one tested policy, not independent evidence. Also, `abs(relative_fitness_drop) <= 0.01` tests change magnitude and discards direction; it does not specifically identify small deteriorations.
## Performance Analysis
- **All four new evaluations fall below every previously summarized program.** Exact scores distinguish Brief Quantum Conversion (**0.3289**), the two equivalent threshold programs (**0.3233** each), and Four-Particle Quantum Burst (**0.3198**). Their mean-error penalties against the best are respectively **+0.2961**, **+0.3484**, and **+0.3821**.
- **The best combines lower average error with lower observed variability.** Its worst-case error/SD are **2.2709/0.4853**, compared with **3.4130/0.9502** for Brief Quantum Conversion, **3.6634/0.8592** for the threshold policy, and **3.3607/0.8186** for Four-Particle Quantum Burst. Brief Quantum Conversion wins four cases against the best, but regressions of **+1.1420 in case_002** and **+1.4386 in case_004** outweigh those gains.
- **Validation success still does not establish evolutionary improvement.** All programs passed validation and completed eight cases, but the best exactly matches reconstructed **5+1**. Against **5+0**, its mean difference is **+0.001084**, with four wins, four losses, and paired-difference SD **0.4535**. These equal-budget results—**500,000 counted queries per case**, one five-dimensional regime—establish development-set rankings, without demonstrating held-out generalization.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Reduce the conditional follow-up to one conversion.** Return five on detection, then one only when `has_detected_change`, `updates_since_detected_change == 1`, and `recent_improvement <= 0`; return zero otherwise. This changes only intensity relative to the strongest tested modification, Conditional Follow-Up, while preserving the best program’s full initial burst.
2. **Preserve one ordinary update only when motion appears promising.** Set `r = max(observation["default_radius"], 1e-12)`; on detection, return four only when `mean_neutral_distance_to_best <= r`, `mean_neutral_speed <= r`, and `mean_velocity_alignment_to_best >= 0.5`, otherwise five, with zero between detections. This tests a geometry-based exception to full conversion; unconditional four performed poorly, and these exploratory conditions cannot select which particle remains ordinary.
3. **Delay the existing conditional rescue by one update.** Keep five conversions on detection, but move the two-conversion rescue from age one to `updates_since_detected_change == 2`, still requiring `has_detected_change` and `recent_improvement <= 0`; return zero otherwise. This changes only the rescue timing relative to Conditional Follow-Up and lets one ordinary neutral-particle update occur before reconsidering exploration.
4. **Test fitness-change direction instead of magnitude.** On detection, return four when `relative_fitness_drop < 0`, otherwise five; return zero between detections. Unlike the unsuccessful absolute 1% threshold, this explicitly preserves full conversion for observed deterioration and tests reduced intervention for observed improvement, without assuming that local fitness direction describes the whole environment.
5. **Try one late rescue restricted to a compact, slow swarm.** Preserve five conversions on detection, then allow one conversion only when `has_detected_change`, age equals eight, `recent_improvement <= 0`, `neutral_diameter <= r`, and `mean_neutral_speed <= 0.1*r`, using the radius normalization above. Return zero elsewhere: this exploratory rule preserves seven intervening ordinary updates and avoids the repeated early intervention associated with unsuccessful tails, although one update without improvement does not establish sustained stagnation.
