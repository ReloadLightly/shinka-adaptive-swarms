## Observed mechanisms and supplied coordination

The historical G26 policy uses public actor IDs to assign target groups, public time to schedule construction and private memory to avoid partners that rejected proposals. Additions require consent; exits do not. Every actor executes the same evolved policy with separate memory. External evolutionary selection evaluates complete policies by terminal population utility relative to the frozen comparator. This is decentralized network execution under a shared convention, not cooperative coevolution of independently evolving actor populations. [G26 source](programs/gen_26/main.java), [actor interface](source/java-paper/agents/PaperSimulation.java).

The hypothetical clique payoff is `(d − c0 − c1)k² + (2 + e − d)k` for an actor with `k` neighbors on both layers. Actors evaluate hypothetical homogeneous populations using their own costs. After a partial shock, the common formula can therefore produce incompatible preferred memberships. Strong triangle rewards favor large cliques. High clustering can reflect one large clique rather than modular organization; the measured concentration of G26 gains in strong-triangle regimes does not establish a general advantage of dense networks.

**Interpretation.** Realized graphs, construction delays and exclusion arise through interactions, whereas cohort boundaries and phase timing are explicitly programmed. Reynolds's flocking model illustrates how programmed local rules can produce collective outcomes; it does not make every supplied convention an emergent institution. Here, the convention evolves in the external search, while actors execute it within each trajectory. [Reynolds, 1987](https://www.red3d.com/cwr/papers/1987/SIGGRAPH87.pdf).

## Construction, maintenance and competing explanations of exclusion

**Observed trajectory.** In case `f4477faf0cefc316a6433134` (LH-26, `d=0.8`, `e=2`), directly shocked actor 34 belongs to a complete overlapping five-actor clique at the time-70 and time-93 snapshots, with utility 6.4. By time 97 it is isolated and remains so at time 100. The summaries establish the local clique topology at both earlier snapshots, not unchanged membership between them. Under G5 and G12 it ends in a complete overlapping five-actor clique with utility 6.4; under the original it ends at utility 6.0. [Retained observations and trajectory hashes](research-team/actor34-evidence.json).

This is loss of an already assembled local structure, not simply failure ever to construct one. Collapse follows G26's return to immediate improvement, but temporal order does not identify causation. Earlier construction paths, available rewiring opportunities and activation order remain rival explanations. Without an action-level trace, we cannot identify the initiating event or claim that disabling the handoff would prevent collapse.

**Observed endpoints.** Two selected adverse intermediate-incentive examples have no remaining edges between high- and low-cost actors, while low-cost actors form large cliques. The selected favorable HL-26 example at `d=1.2`, `e=1.6` instead includes all forty actors. Actor 34 shares its programmed block with low-cost actors 30–32, so its exclusion is not explained entirely by ID boundaries. Exact saved-graph arithmetic shows that actor 34 would gain 0.4 from linking to actor 30, while actor 30 would lose 4.4 and refuse under the final original rule. This is a counterfactual calculation, not a recorded offer. Endogenous access barriers are plausible alongside imposed groups; selected examples do not estimate prevalence. [Cost-mixing evidence](research-team/cost-mixing-evidence.json), [critical review](research-team/critical-connections.md).

The formation–maintenance distinction connects directly to the source literature. Smaldino, D'Souza and Maoz describe structural entrenchment: relationships constructed under earlier incentives can remain viable after costs change. Burghardt and Maoz extend that framework to partial shocks and noisy exploration. The evolved policy adds deliberate construction under a common convention, followed by its withdrawal. The collapse case asks when a built structure becomes sustainable under ordinary local choices; it does not refute entrenchment, because the policies and conditions differ. [Smaldino, D'Souza and Maoz, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1017/nws.2017.35), [Burghardt and Maoz, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-31960-y).

## Collective gains, individual stability and temporal costs

**Observed tradeoffs.** Historical G26 improves mean terminal utility over G12 by 0.858264 units, while terminal-loss observations against the original rise from 5 to 287 and isolates from 3 to 105. Losses use differences below −1e−9. The counts cover 11,520 dependent actor-case observations; count differences need not identify the same actors changing status. G12 remains an alternative with fewer terminal losses. G26 remains the winner at generation 30 under the frozen objective. Network efficiency and individual stability are distinct theoretical concepts; neither a high score nor a finite cleanup phase proves stability. [Jackson and Wolinsky, 1996](https://doi.org/10.1006/jeth.1996.0108).

Endpoint gains also need not repay the path. In the documented case, G12 leaves actor 34 with terminal utility 0.4 above the original but accumulated utility 209.6 lower. Across the panel, G26 has 1,531 cumulative-loss observations versus 287 terminal losses at the same tolerance. Accumulated utility is an undiscounted sum of saved round-level payoffs, not a financial balance. The model has no financing, survival or credit constraint. **Interpretation:** liquidity research identifies a missing feasibility question; it does not establish that the simulated construction process is unaffordable or diagnose a financial crisis. [Holmström and Tirole, 1998](https://doi.org/10.1086/250001).

## Interpretation: cooperation under international anarchy

Absence of superior authority need not imply disorder. Bilateral consent, unilateral exit, repeated interaction and partner choice make this an abstract setting for cooperation without a central edge allocator. However, common deployment installs the same costly-acceptance convention. It does not explain how independently strategic governments negotiate, adopt or enforce it. Coordination, bargaining, credible commitment and enforcement remain separate questions. [Axelrod and Keohane, 1985](https://websites.umich.edu/~axe/Axelrod%20and%20Keohane%20Coop%20.pdf), [Fearon, 1998](https://www.web.stanford.edu/group/fearon-research/cgi-bin/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/Bargaining-Enforcement-and-International-Cooperation.pdf).

Rejection memory is not demonstrated reputation or reciprocal punishment. Unequal payoffs do not instantiate preferences over relative gains, military vulnerability or strategic rivalry. An actor's interest in another actor's relative advantage is absent from the payoff function. Consequently, the experiment does not verify structural realism. Network scholarship motivates examining indirect dependence and multilayer relations while acknowledging these missing political mechanisms. [Snidal, 1991](https://doi.org/10.2307/1963847), [Maoz, 2010](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/networks-of-nations/D648F66AF4B78F8E086C931618C2CC52).

Japan's Indo-Pacific partnerships motivate questions about cross-domain reinforcement, partner choice and unequal exposure; they do not calibrate these actors to states. Weaponized interdependence requires asymmetric network positions and institutional or jurisdictional authority over access through consequential hubs. Those mechanisms are absent here. Connections to diplomacy and coercion remain research hypotheses, not empirical findings. [Japan MOFA](https://www.mofa.go.jp/policy/pageite_000001_01612.html), [Farrell and Newman, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/ISEC_a_00351).

## Future work — proposed, not executed

A minimal maintenance diagnostic would compare G26 with a variant differing only in its time-93 handoff, using the same initial state, case, environment inputs and random seeds while recording the first divergence. Subsequent action-RNG consumption may differ because behavior changes. A separate one-actor deviation could test whether costly acceptance is individually advantageous. Relabeling public membership IDs while preserving physical histories would test dependence on identity conventions. These answer different questions and should not be bundled into one intervention.

For broader claims, predeclared fresh history families should compare the selected policy, G12 and the original on terminal utility, cumulative losses, isolation and shock/control contrasts. Name the objective winner and distributional comparator before observing those results. Further optimization on the same development panel cannot substitute for independent confirmation. None of these studies is part of the present continuation.
