<DESCRIPTION>
The stronger relocation and velocity reset increased mean error from 2.31 to 2.33 without resolving the worst case. Remove that escalation and test a different recovery mechanism: reset memories only when severe deterioration, compact geometry, and stalled progress coincide. The hypothesis is that concentrated historical attractors can delay recovery after a substantial change; releasing them may help retained particles explore while preserving velocity and the parent's bounded relocation radius. Otherwise, reevaluate memories to preserve useful search history. Memory refresh consumes less than 1% of evaluations, so the intended benefit is improved recovery rather than evaluation savings. This proposal requires measurement; the existing feedback does not establish memory retention as the cause of tracking failure.
</DESCRIPTION>
