# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **G21, Spread and Fitness Loss Gated Relocation, replaces G14 as best:** mean error **3.524052**, score **0.221041**, versus G14’s **3.528668 / 0.220815**. Its capped coverage gate retains radius multiplier **1.5** and predominantly relocates four particles. The mean-error improvement is **0.13%**, updating the previous best-program finding.
- **Fixed radius 1.5 with a selective three-particle exception remains the strongest pattern.** The leading order is **G21 > G14 > G23 > G19** by score. G23’s stricter `diameter > 3.5R` rule achieves **3.547793 / 0.219887**, placing it ahead of G19’s **3.573097 / 0.218670**, but behind G21 and G14.
- **G21 extends the measured advantage over earlier controls:** mean error is **6.11% lower than G6**, **7.56% lower than fixed-four G2/3**, and **15.28% lower than corrected baseline G0**. These are search-suite improvements; the upstream correction itself remains baseline work.
## Ineffective Approaches
- **Conditional radius expansion continues to underperform.** G20 combines a broader three-particle eligibility boundary, `diameter > 2.5R`, with compact/high-loss expansion to **1.75**. Its mean **3.966419**, score **0.201352**, is **12.55% worse in mean error than G21**. This reinforces the earlier G12/G15 expansion results, although G20 changes both allocation and radius, preventing attribution to either alone.
- **G22’s displacement-adaptive radius does not improve the leading policies.** Four particles with multipliers **1.125–1.5** produce mean **3.795143**, score **0.208544**, and worst case **11.9043**. It slightly improves mean error over fixed-four/radius-1.5 G2/3 (**3.812099**), while worsening its worst case (**9.1811**). This echoes G17’s smaller-radius tradeoff.
- **G24’s conserved radial effort does not preserve tracking quality.** Increasing allocation to five while shrinking the multiplier to approximately **1.342** yields mean **4.059007**, score **0.197667**, and worst case **13.2360**—the weakest new result. Five-particle responses occur **2,143/3,320 times (64.55%)**; this branch substantially changes behavior. Its case_015 interval-end error **11.97**, versus G21’s **6.02**, shows poorer recovery without isolating the cause.
## Implementation Insights
- **G21 combines G19’s curved threshold with G14’s loss ceiling.** For positive default radius \(R\), it permits three particles only when \(d>3R\) and loss is at most `min(0.075, 0.10*(1-(3R/d)**2))`. Between **3R and 6R**, it is stricter than G14; at **6R and above**, it matches G14 and excludes G19’s more permissive high-spread decisions.
- **The best policy’s exception remains rare:** G21 selects three particles in **224/3,350 responses (6.69%)**, compared with G14’s **6.88%**, G19’s **6.83%**, and G23’s **6.44%**. Similar frequencies accompany different errors, so frequency alone does not explain success. G21’s response-weighted allocated fraction is **78.66%**, with absolute radii **0.75** and **2.25**.
- **Execution accounting does not explain the ranking.** Requested and allocated counts match, with no horizon truncation. Memory reevaluation and retained velocities are shared mechanisms; non-relocated particles still receive ordinary PSO evaluations. Detection consumes roughly **16%** of evaluations, but these diagnostics establish neither direct query savings from partial relocation nor a causal explanation for performance.
## Performance Analysis
- **G21’s lead over G14 is concentrated in one case.** Saved metrics show improvement only in **case_004**, by **0.073860** offline error; the other **15 cases are unchanged**. The severity-1/interval-5000 regime mean falls **1.678774 → 1.660309**. Thus, the aggregate improvement is narrow, despite establishing the new measured best.
- **G21’s advantage over G19 is broader but still mixed:** five cases improve, one worsens, and ten remain unchanged, reducing mean error **1.37%**. Against G23, G21 improves case_002 by **0.552764**, worsens case_001 by **0.172902**, and leaves fourteen unchanged.

All retain worst-case error **8.895466** and case_015 interval-end error **6.0203**. - **All five new programs passed validation and completed 16 cases.** The score `1/(1+mean_error)` ranks mean error only; rounded **0.22** scores conceal differences among the leaders. # META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Refine G21’s curved loss boundary.** Keep its `diameter > 3R` gate, `0.075` ceiling, four-particle default, and radius multiplier `1.5`; separately replace `0.10 * coverage` with `0.0875 * coverage` and `0.1125 * coverage`, where `R = default_radius`. G21’s narrow improvement over G14 supports testing nearby degrees of restriction without assuming that stricter is always better.
2. **Tune the loss ceiling independently.** Create separate G21 mutations using `min(0.0625, 0.10 * coverage)` and `min(0.0875, 0.10 * coverage)`, preserving every other decision. G21’s advantage over uncapped G19 supports retaining a ceiling, while leaving its best value unresolved.
3. **Adjust the spread boundary around G21’s successful setting.** Replace `reference_diameter = 3.0 * R` with `2.75 * R` and `3.25 * R` in separate variants, using the replacement consistently in both the early return and coverage calculation. G23’s competitive but weaker stricter gate motivates a smaller adjustment; retain the `0.075` ceiling, three/four allocation, and radius `1.5`.
4. **Bracket the fixed radius without changing allocation.** Preserve G21’s exact count rule and test constant multipliers `1.4375` and `1.5625` separately. The repeated success of `1.5`, alongside poorer adaptive-radius and expansion results, makes small constant perturbations a better-supported next step than another broad radius response.
5. **Add a progress-supported preservation exception.** Retain G21’s existing three-particle branch, then additionally allow `min(3, swarm_size)` when `diameter > 6R`, `0.075 < relative_fitness_drop <= 0.0875`, and `recent_improvement > max(fitness_drop, 0.0)`; otherwise retain four particles and radius `1.5`. This tests whether observed progress can identify useful exceptions to the ceiling, addressing G19’s unsuccessful unconditional permissiveness while treating the benefit of progress as an unproven hypothesis.
