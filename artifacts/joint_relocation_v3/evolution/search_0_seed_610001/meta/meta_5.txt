# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **The current best, Fixed Four-Particle Relocation with Expanded Radius, combines count 4 with radius multiplier 1.5.** It achieved score **0.20781**, mean error **3.8121**, and worst-case error **9.18**. Its mean error was **8.4% lower** than the corrected full-swarm baseline and **6.2% lower** than full-swarm relocation at the same expanded radius.
- **Moderate radius expansion helped full-swarm relocation.** Increasing the multiplier from 1.0 to 1.5 reduced mean error from **4.1596 to 4.0635** and increased score from **0.19381 to 0.19749**. Worst-case error also decreased, from **12.92 to 12.06**.
- **Partial relocation improved the observed error distribution.** At multiplier 1.5, both four-particle and three-particle relocation outperformed five-particle relocation on mean error. Three-particle relocation achieved the lowest worst-case error (**8.21**) and case-error standard deviation (**1.86**), while four-particle relocation retained the best mean.
## Ineffective Approaches
- **Geometry-Threshold Joint Relocation underperformed every tested constant policy on mean error.** Its diameter threshold selected either three particles at multiplier 1.5 or five at multiplier 2.0, yielding mean error **4.3315** and score **0.18756**—approximately **13.6% higher error than the current best**. Because both count and radius changed between branches, these results cannot isolate which choice caused the deterioration.
- **Reducing allocation from four particles to three sacrificed average performance.** At the same multiplier, mean error increased from **3.8121 to 3.9490**, lowering score from **0.20781 to 0.20206**. The improvement in worst-case error and dispersion therefore represents a tradeoff, rather than uniform superiority.
- **Expanded radius alone did not resolve persistent tracking errors.** Full-swarm relocation’s case_015 interval-end error increased from **10.46 to 10.76** despite its better overall mean. Geometry dispatch reached **11.01** on that diagnostic. Aggregate improvement did not imply improved recovery in every case.
## Implementation Insights
- **The best program’s effective behavior is a constant joint setting:** `count=min(4, int(observation["swarm_size"]))`, `radius_scale=1.5`. Four particles undergo relocation while one continues ordinary PSO motion. This combination performed best without using geometry, fitness deterioration, or recovery history; the evaluations do not establish a benefit from state dependence.
- **Allocation encoding was faithful.** For the best program, requested and allocated counts were consistently four, actual allocation was **80%**, and adapter fraction **0.7** encoded that integer through the ceiling rule. No responses were horizon-truncated, so its advantage is not explained by requests being shortened at the evaluation limit.
- **Lower relocation count does not directly eliminate particle evaluations.** Non-relocated particles still move and receive evaluations; all personal memories are reevaluated and velocities retained across these policies. Those shared mechanisms—including baseline corrections—cannot be credited as evolutionary improvements. Absolute radii remain severity-scaled: **0.75** or **2.25** for the best program.
## Performance Analysis
- **Mean-error ranking was four-particle, three-particle, expanded full-swarm, baseline full-swarm, then geometry dispatch:** **3.8121, 3.9490, 4.0635, 4.1596, 4.3315**. The reciprocal score ranks only mean error; lower worst-case error or standard deviation receives no separate reward.
- **The best program still struggled with larger movements.** Its four-case mean errors were **3.12 / 2.06** for severity 1 at intervals 2500 / 5000, versus **5.59 / 4.49** for severity 3. Longer intervals coincided with lower group means, although these aggregates do not isolate interval length as the cause.
- **Two cases concentrated the remaining difficulty.** Cases **010 and 015** had offline errors **9.18 and 8.82**, accounting for approximately **29.5%** of summed case error. Their interval-end errors, **5.88 and 6.02**, show substantial tracking gaps persisting until the next change.
- **Execution correctness and search performance are distinct.** All five programs passed checks across 16 cases. The best program spent **15.25–16.08%** of evaluations on detection and **0.29–1.93%** on memory refresh. These observations support the reported search ranking, but neither identify the causal mechanism nor establish generalization.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Refine radius while retaining four-particle relocation.** Keep `count=min(4, int(observation["swarm_size"]))` and try constant multipliers `1.25`, `1.375`, `1.625`, and `1.75` as separate mutations. Four particles at `1.5` achieved the best mean error, but the optimal radius for that allocation remains unexplored.
2. **Retune radius specifically for three-particle relocation.** Try `count=min(3, swarm_size)` with multipliers `1.25` and `1.75`. Three particles at `1.5` produced the lowest worst-case error and dispersion; adjusting its radius could recover some of the mean-performance gap without assuming the four-particle optimum transfers.
3. **Introduce a conservative three-versus-four allocation rule.** Hold radius at `1.5`, choose three particles when `relative_fitness_drop <= τ`, and otherwise retain four, trying `τ=0`, `0.05`, and `0.10` separately. This tests whether preserving two ordinary PSO movers helps after mild observed deterioration, building on three-particle relocation’s favorable tail errors while avoiding the failed policy’s simultaneous radius switch.
4. **Use observed displacement for bounded radius adjustment.** Keep four particles and define `q=max(0, observed_best_displacement)/max(default_radius, 1e-12)`, then set `radius_scale=1.5+0.25*min(1, max(0, q-1))`. This preserves the best setting for small observed displacements and cautiously expands toward `1.75` for larger ones; persistent tracking errors motivate the hypothesis, although observed displacement is not true peak movement.
5. **Condition modest radius expansion on weak recent recovery.** Keep four particles and use multiplier `1.75` only when `fitness_drop > 0` and `max(0, recent_improvement) < 0.25*fitness_drop`; otherwise return `1.5`, with `0.5` as a separate threshold mutation. This tests whether expansion helps when recent gains are small relative to newly observed deterioration, while preserving the successful constant response elsewhere and treating state dependence as unproven.
