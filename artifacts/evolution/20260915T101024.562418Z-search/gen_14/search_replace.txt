<DESCRIPTION>
Reducing relocation improved mean offline error from 2.31 to 1.98, supporting a further test of spatial preservation. For mild observed deterioration, relocate one of five particles instead of two. Keep the three-particle response for stronger deterioration, and allow dispersed swarms to retain more particles because they already provide spatial coverage.
The hypothesis is that small changes often leave useful particle positions intact: one relocated scout can initiate recovery while four particles continue local tracking. Preserve memory reevaluation, velocities, and the existing radius rule. Memory refresh costs only about 0.4–0.6% of evaluations in the supplied results, providing little budget motivation for discarding memories. This proposal remains untested; its main risk is slower recovery when a small fitness drop masks substantial peak movement.
