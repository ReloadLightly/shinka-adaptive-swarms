# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Generation 14, Conditional Particle Relocation, is the new best:** mean offline error **3.5287**, score **0.220815**, versus Generation 6’s **3.7534 / 0.210375**. Raising the relative-drop threshold from **0.05 to 0.075**, while retaining the diameter condition and radius multiplier **1.5**, reduces mean error by **5.99%**.
- **Predominantly four-particle relocation with occasional three-particle responses remains successful.** Generation 14 selects three in **230 of 3,341 responses (6.88%)**, compared with Generation 6’s **6.23%**. Four-particle responses still account for **93.12%**; the gain accompanies a modest change in overall allocation frequency.
- **Conditional allocation improves upon fixed-four/radius-1.5 Generations 2/3:** Generation 14 reduces mean error by **7.44%**, worst-case error from **9.18 to 8.90**, and standard deviation from **2.28 to 1.88**. Its mean error is also **15.17% below Generation 0’s corrected baseline**, although that comparison changes both allocation and radius.
## Ineffective Approaches
- **Loss-triggered full relocation underperforms.** Generation 11’s `selective_full_relocation` produces mean error **4.04**, score **0.198404**; Generation 13’s `tiered_loss_response` produces **4.12 / 0.195349**. Their aggressive allocations accompany case_015 interval-end errors **10.78** and **10.75**, versus Generation 14’s **6.02**. These observations establish poorer tracking, without identifying its cause.
- **Conditional radius expansion does not improve Generation 6’s mean objective.** Generation 12 retains its allocation rule but raises the multiplier to **1.75** for compact, deteriorating swarms. Mean error worsens from **3.7534 to 3.7929**, and score falls to **0.208640**, although standard deviation improves slightly from **2.01 to 1.97**.
- **Displacement adaptation remains unconvincing.** Generation 10’s damped **1.125–1.375** multiplier improves upon Generations 7/9’s mean error (**3.81 versus 3.90**), but its worst-case error increases to **12.11**. It narrowly improves fixed-four/radius-1.5’s mean (**3.8096 versus 3.8121**) while substantially worsening its worst case (**12.11 versus 9.18**); there is no consistent advantage across metrics.
- Earlier limitations persist: fixed radius **1.375** in Generation 8 yields mean error **4.10**, while always relocating three in Generations 4/5 yields **3.95**. However, Generations 4/5 still have the lowest worst-case error (**8.21**) and standard deviation (**1.86**), so Generation 14 does not dominate every metric.
## Implementation Insights
- **Generation 14’s successful change is narrowly defined:** choose three instead of four when `relative_fitness_drop <= 0.075` **and** `swarm_diameter > 3 * default_radius`. Relative to Generation 6, the decision differs only for sufficiently spread swarms with drops in **(0.05, 0.075]**. The resulting trajectory changes support the complete policy’s search improvement, without establishing a causal benefit from state dependence.
- **A fixed multiplier still produces severity-scaled relocation.** Generation 14’s multiplier **1.5** gives absolute radii **0.75** and **2.25**. Its radius is constant within each severity setting; allocation supplies the adaptation to observed state.
- **Exact integer allocation is reflected in execution diagnostics.** Generation 14’s response-weighted allocation is **78.62%**, versus Generation 6’s **78.75%**. Requested and allocated counts match, with no horizon truncation. Adapter encoding fractions are not allocation fractions.
- **Memory reevaluation and retained velocities are shared mechanisms**, not evolved improvements. Non-relocated particles still receive ordinary PSO evaluations, so lower relocation counts do not directly save those queries. Generation 14’s introductory baseline docstring remains stale; its executable rule defines its behavior.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Refine the successful fitness-drop threshold.** Starting from Generation 14, test `0.0875` and `0.10` as separate replacements for `0.075`, keeping the diameter condition and radius multiplier `1.5` unchanged. The previous threshold increase reduced mean error by 5.99%, making a further modest extension the strongest immediate lead, although improvement need not continue monotonically.
2. **Tune the geometry boundary independently.** Keep the drop threshold `0.075` and test `swarm_diameter > k * default_radius` with `k=2.5` and `k=3.5` in separate mutations. Generation 14’s three-particle branch activates only 6.88% of responses, so these variants test whether admitting moderately tighter swarms or requiring greater spread improves that selective allocation.
3. **Use recent progress to permit a narrow additional three-particle branch.** Preserve Generation 14’s existing behavior, but also choose three when `0.075 < relative_fitness_drop <= 0.10`, the existing diameter condition holds, and `recent_improvement > max(fitness_drop, 0.0)`. Keep radius `1.5`; this extends the successful preservation pattern only when observed progress exceeds deterioration, a hypothesis whose benefit remains untested.
4. **Explore slightly larger fixed radii with Generation 14’s allocation intact.** Create separate variants using constant `radius_scale=1.5625` and `1.625`, changing no count conditions. The strong results around `1.5`, weaker fixed `1.375`, and unsuccessful conditional expansion to `1.75` favor a small local search over another large or displacement-driven adjustment.
5. **Test radius and allocation coupling only in the preservation branch.** Keep Generation 14’s exact count rule, but use `radius_scale=1.625` for three-particle responses and `1.5` for four-particle responses. This tests whether slightly broader relocation complements preserving two ordinary PSO particles while retaining the dominant successful response; unlike the unsuccessful compact-swarm expansion, it targets broad swarms with limited observed loss.
