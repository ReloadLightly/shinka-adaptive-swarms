# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Fine-tune radius while retaining four particles.** Keep `count=min(4, int(observation["swarm_size"]))` and try separate constant multipliers `0.75`, `0.9`, `1.1`, and `1.25`. Four particles currently win at radius `1.0`, but radius changes have only been evaluated with full allocation, leaving this promising neighborhood unexplored.
2. **Switch conservatively between three and four particles.** Keep radius `1.0` and select three when `relative_fitness_drop <= t`, otherwise four, trying thresholds `t=0.0`, `0.05`, and `0.1` in separate mutations; clamp each count to swarm size. This combines the two strongest constant policies, testing whether smaller observed deterioration identifies situations where three particles’ better extremes can complement four particles’ lower mean error.
3. **Adapt radius smoothly to observed deterioration.** Retain four particles and try `radius_scale = 1.0 + 0.5*d/(d+0.1)`, where `d=max(0.0, observation["relative_fitness_drop"])`. This preserves the winning baseline radius for negligible deterioration and caps expansion at `1.5`, testing selective widening given the mixed results of unconditional larger radii.
4. **Condition allocation on existing swarm spread.** Define `q=swarm_diameter/max(default_radius, 1e-12)` and use three particles when `q>T`, otherwise four, with radius fixed at `1.0`; try `T=2`, `4`, and `8` separately. Both partial allocations already outperform full allocation on mean error, making spread a concrete candidate signal for choosing between them, although its predictive value remains unproven.
5. **Add a narrowly triggered joint recovery response.** Default to `(count=4, radius_scale=1.0)`, but try `(3, 1.5)` when `relative_fitness_drop>0.1` and `swarm_diameter<2*default_radius`, clamping the count to swarm size. Three particles and wider full relocation separately improved the difficult case’s interval-end diagnostic; combining fewer relocations with wider placement tests a new interaction while retaining the best policy elsewhere, without treating that diagnostic as proof of the failure mechanism.
