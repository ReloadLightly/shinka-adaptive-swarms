# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

**Successful Algorithmic Patterns**
- **Generation 8 remains best:** fixed `count=min(4, swarm_size)` and `radius_scale=1.25` achieve score **0.210153**, mean error **3.7584**. Its error is **9.64% below** the corrected full-swarm baseline’s **4.1596** and **7.51% below** full-swarm relocation at radius 1.5. None of Generations 10–14 improves its mean.
- **Four-particle allocation succeeds across two tested radii.** At radius 1.5, Generation 5 achieves mean **3.8121**, versus **4.0635** for five particles. Reducing its radius to Generation 8’s 1.25 improves mean error **1.41%**, improving **11/16 cases**.
- **Lowering the displacement-adaptive radius band helps that policy modestly.** Generation 12’s 1.25–1.50 band achieves **0.203912 / 3.9041**, versus Generations 7/9’s **0.202248 / 3.9444** with 1.50–1.75: a **1.02%** mean-error reduction. This remains weaker than both leading constants.
**Ineffective Approaches**
- **Intermediate radius expansion worsens the four-particle constant.** Generation 10’s radius 1.375 scores **0.195942**, with mean **4.1035**, **9.18% above Generation 8**. Worst-case error and standard deviation also increase to **12.79 / 2.97**. Since radius 1.5 performs better than 1.375, these results show no monotonic radius–performance relationship.
- **Observed-state expansion has not beaten the constants.** Generation 12’s displacement rule increases mean error **3.87%** relative to Generation 8; Generation 14’s fitness-drop/recovery rule increases it **5.41%**, scoring **0.201536 / 3.9619**. Earlier Generation 6 likewise underperformed. Their triggers execute valid policies, but the feedback does not establish why their selected expansions hurt aggregate performance.
- **Reducing allocation to three worsens the scored objective.** Generations 11/13 at radius 1.25 achieve **0.199622 / 4.0095**, **6.68% higher mean error** than Generation 8. They also underperform the earlier three-particle radius-1.5 policy’s **0.202063 / 3.9490**; the favorable radius reduction observed with four particles does not transfer to three.
- **Joint geometry-threshold switching remains the weakest reported mean result:** three particles at radius 1.5 versus five at radius 2.0 produces **0.187564 / 4.3315**, **15.25% above Generation 8**. Changing allocation and radius together prevents attributing this deficit to either component alone.
**Implementation Insights**
- **Generation 8’s successful mechanism is a constant action pair.** It relocates four of five particles at absolute radius **0.625 or 1.875**, leaving one particle to ordinary PSO movement. It uses swarm capacity without adapting to fitness loss, displacement, recovery, or remaining budget; its performance does not establish beneficial state dependence.
- **Integer encoding and actual allocation differ.** The adapter’s fraction **0.7** encodes four particles, corresponding to **80% allocation**; **0.5** encodes three, corresponding to **60%**. Recorded allocations match requests, with no horizon truncation, so these comparisons do not reflect allocation-rounding failures.
- **Shared simulator behavior is not an evolved improvement.** Memory reevaluation and retained velocities apply throughout, and non-relocated particles still move and consume evaluations. Generation 8’s advantage cannot be credited to removing those evaluations or correcting upstream behavior.
- **Equivalent behavior is not independent discovery.** Generations 11 and 13 implement the same three-particle/radius-1.25 policy and produce identical outcomes, paralleling Generations 7/9. Generation 12 actually uses `max(0, swarm_size-1)`, equivalent to four particles in these tests but different from `min(4, swarm_size)` at other capacities.
**Performance Analysis**
- **Rounded scores conceal meaningful differences.** Exact artifact scores rank the new programs as Generation 12 **0.203912**, Generation 14 **0.201536**, Generations 11/13 **0.199622**, and Generation 10 **0.195942**—all below Generation 8’s **0.210153**. The transformation `1/(1+mean_error)` rewards mean error alone.
- **Generation 8’s mean advantage carries a tail tradeoff.** Generations 11/13 reduce worst-case error from **12.15 to 9.05** and standard deviation from **2.60 to 1.97**, reductions of **25.48%** and **24.31%**, despite their worse mean. Earlier three-particle relocation at radius 1.5 has still lower values, **8.21 / 1.86**.
- **Case_015 remains consequential but does not determine the ranking alone.** It contributes **20.2%** of Generation 8’s summed case error and retains interval-end error **10.84**. Generations 11/13, 12, and 14 lower that endpoint to **7.23**, **9.77**, and **10.49**, respectively, while worsening overall mean error.
- **Validation success and search performance remain distinct.** All five new programs complete 16 cases and pass validation. Detection consumes approximately **16%** of objective evaluations; memory refresh is additional counted overhead. These results establish the observed search ranking, without establishing causal mechanisms or generalization to fresh cases.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Refine the best constant radius first.** Keep `count=min(4, int(observation["swarm_size"]))` and try constant `radius_scale` values `1.125`, `1.1875`, and `1.3125` as separate mutations. Four particles remain strongest across two tested radii; the poor result at `1.375` makes the untested lower neighborhood a higher priority without establishing that further contraction will help.
2. **Preserve a second ordinary PSO mover only after mild deterioration.** Hold `radius_scale=1.25` and request three particles when `relative_fitness_drop <= τ`, otherwise four, testing `τ=0`, `0.025`, and `0.05` separately and capping counts by swarm size. Three-particle constants reduced worst-case error but worsened mean error, motivating selective use while leaving the best action as the default for larger losses.
3. **Replace smooth displacement expansion with a threshold between successful constants.** Keep four particles, compute `q=max(0, observed_best_displacement)/max(default_radius, 1e-12)`, and choose radius `1.5` only when `q>T`, otherwise `1.25`, trying `T=2` and `T=3` separately. This uses the two strongest constant radii while avoiding intermediate values; Generation 12’s weaker interpolation motivates the experiment but does not establish that displacement is predictive.
4. **Test conditional contraction after observed recovery.** Keep four particles and use radius `1.1875` only when `fitness_drop>0` and `max(0, recent_improvement)>=fitness_drop`; otherwise retain `1.25`. The improvement from radius `1.5` to `1.25` motivates a modest contraction hypothesis, while this reverses the expansion direction explored by unsuccessful feedback policies.
5. **Retune the more robust three-particle policy around its own preferred radius.** Request `min(3, swarm_size)` and test constant radii `1.4375` and `1.5625` separately. Three particles performed better at `1.5` than at `1.25` and produced the lowest reported worst-case error, so this branch could improve its mean without assuming that the four-particle radius preference transfers.
