# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **G11, Spread-Adaptive Partial Relocation, remains best:** score **0.214212**, mean offline error **3.6683**. Its rule—three particles when normalized diameter exceeds four, otherwise four, with radius multiplier **1.25**—reduces mean error **2.40%** versus fixed-four G9 (**0.210153 / 3.7584**) and **11.81%** versus corrected baseline G0 (**0.193814 / 4.1596**).
- **Allocation conditioning with a constant radius remains the strongest observed family.** G18, Displacement-Conditioned Relocation Allocation, becomes second-best at **0.213608 / 3.6815**, ahead of G12’s threshold-eight rule (**0.212042 / 3.7161**). G18 preserves G11’s three-or-four allocation and radius **1.25**, but its displacement-dependent threshold adds no improvement over G11.
- **Predominantly four-particle relocation with occasional reductions outperforms the tested constant alternatives.** At radius **1.25**, G11 beats fixed-four G9, fixed-three G14 (**0.199622 / 4.0095**) and full-swarm G5 (**0.199277 / 4.0181**). The evidence supports this particular policy combination on the search suite; it does not isolate a causal benefit from observing current spread.
## Ineffective Approaches
- **Displacement-adaptive radius weakens fixed-four relocation.** G15 scores **0.202028**, with error **3.9498**, versus G9’s **0.210153 / 3.7584**: **5.09% higher error**. Its case-average multipliers remain near 1.25 (**1.233–1.273**), yet worst-case error rises **12.1501 → 13.0845**. Similar average radii therefore do not imply equivalent outcomes.
- **Shrinking the radius for compact swarms substantially worsens G11’s policy.** G19 retains G11’s allocation threshold but uses **1.125** instead of **1.25** in the four-particle branch. Score falls to **0.197054**, mean error rises **11.08%** to **4.0748**, and case_015 interval-end error rises **7.24 → 10.22**. This implementation change performs worse; the diagnostics do not establish the underlying tracking mechanism.
- **Neither lowering nor bounding the spread threshold improves G11.** G16’s threshold of three produces **0.209816 / 3.7661**, **2.67% higher error**. G17’s restriction to `(4, 8]` produces **0.202851 / 3.9297**, **7.13% higher error**, and also trails fixed-four G9. Its three-particle branch activates only **19 of 3,376 responses (0.56%)**.
- **Earlier results still contradict a generally beneficial radius or fitness-loss rule.** G13’s conditional radius expansion trails G9; G14’s fixed-three radius **1.25** trails G1’s radius **1.0**; and G10’s fitness-loss switch (**0.198339 / 4.0419**) trails both fixed-three and fixed-four policies at radius **1.0**. All passed validation, so these are performance limitations rather than execution failures.
## Implementation Insights
- **G11 changes allocation alone using a simple normalized observation.** It computes `swarm_diameter / max(default_radius, 1e-12)`, applies the strict threshold `> 4`, clamps the integer count to swarm size, and holds radius at **1.25**. Four-particle responses account for **2,951/3,440 (85.78%)**; three-particle responses account for **14.22%**.
- **Additional displacement dependence offers no measured advantage here.** G18 replaces G11’s threshold with `4 + 2*u/(1+u)`, where `u` is nonnegative normalized observed displacement. It produces identical case errors to G11 in **10 of 16 cases**, improves two and worsens four. G15 instead changes radius through `1.375 - 0.25/(1+u)`; its numerical safeguards support execution but do not translate into better optimization.
- **Allocation counts and evaluation costs remain distinct.** Three and four particles mean actual fractions **0.6 and 0.8**; adapter fractions **0.5 and 0.7** merely encode those counts. Non-relocated particles still undergo ordinary PSO motion. Memory reevaluation and retained velocities are shared controls, not evolved improvements. Requested, allocated and objectively queried relocation counts match, with no reported horizon truncation.
## Performance Analysis
- **The updated leading order is G11 > G18 > G12 > G9 > G16.** G18 misses G11 by only **0.0132 mean error (0.36%)**, whereas G15, G17 and G19 exceed G11’s error by **7.68%, 7.13% and 11.08%**, respectively. Rounded scores of **0.20–0.21** conceal these differences.
- **G11’s advantage remains uneven across cases.** Against G9, it improves seven cases and worsens nine. The **2.7271** reduction in case_015 exceeds its total paired reduction across all 16 cases (**1.4426**); excluding that case, G11 averages **3.2846**, versus G9’s **3.1990**. G18 leaves case_015 unchanged and adds small aggregate losses elsewhere.
- **Mean and tail rankings differ.** G16 has slightly lower case-error dispersion than G11 (**2.0671 versus 2.0817**) despite worse mean error; both have worst-case error **9.4230**. G14 retains the lower worst-case error **9.0543** despite substantially worse mean performance. Selection uses `1/(1+mean_error)`, so neither tail error nor dispersion receives independent weight.
- **Correctness and search performance do not establish generalization.** All five new programs passed all 16 cases. G11, G16 and G18 still leave case_015 interval-end error at **7.2366**. Detection and memory costs are counted, but their shares do not explain the ranking; repeated use of this search suite also limits claims about independent performance or the value of state dependence.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Refine the spread threshold above four.** Keep `q = swarm_diameter / max(default_radius, 1e-12)` and radius `1.25`, but try `count = 3 if q > T else 4` with `T=4.5` and `6.0` as separate mutations, preserving the count clamp. Threshold three underperformed, while threshold eight and G18 remained competitive, making intermediate thresholds a reasonable next step.
2. **Shrink radius only for three-particle responses.** Preserve G11’s allocation rule and set `radius_scale=1.125` or `1.0` when `q>4`, retaining `1.25` otherwise. Fixed-three relocation performed better at radius `1.0` than `1.25`; this tests whether that interaction transfers to G11’s dispersed branch while preserving its dominant four-particle behavior.
3. **Try a small radius increase only for four-particle responses.** Keep three-particle responses at `1.25`, and separately test `1.3125` and `1.375` when `q<=4`. This branch accounts for approximately 86% of G11’s responses, and G19 demonstrates sensitivity to its radius, although the failure of shrinking does not establish that expansion will help.
4. **Fine-tune a single constant radius while preserving adaptive allocation.** Retain G11’s exact threshold and counts, but use `radius_scale=1.1875` or `1.3125` for every response in separate candidates. Constant-radius allocation policies form the strongest observed family, so these small changes test whether `1.25` is locally optimal without adding another state-dependent rule.
5. **Try an observation-independent three/four allocation mixture.** At constant radius `1.25`, choose three particles with probability `0.1422` and four otherwise, using a candidate-local RNG independent of the environment RNG and preserving the count clamp. This approximates G11’s observed allocation frequency and could retain useful occasional reductions without its spread trigger; G11’s uneven advantage over fixed-four leaves the benefit of that trigger unresolved.
