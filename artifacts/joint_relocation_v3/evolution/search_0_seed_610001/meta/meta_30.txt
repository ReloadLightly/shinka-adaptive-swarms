# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **Generation 22, `four_particle_radius_blend`, remains the best evaluated program:** score **0.215323**, mean error **3.6442**. Its constant `count=min(4, int(swarm_size))` and radius multiplier **1.21875** outperform every new candidate. The earlier improvement over Generation 17’s multiplier **1.1875** remains **2.79% lower mean error**.
- **Generation 29 isolates a successful allocation change at the baseline radius.** Four particles at multiplier **1.0** achieve score **0.208903**, mean error **3.7869**, versus the corrected five-particle baseline’s **0.193814**, **4.1596**: an **8.96% error reduction** on these search cases. Generation 22’s radius adjustment lowers error another **3.77%** relative to Generation 29.
- **Generation 27 improves tail outcomes, although it loses on the selection objective.** Its displacement-dependent radius achieves worst-case error **10.8808** and standard deviation **2.4852**, below Generation 22’s **12.2207** and **2.5896**. Its mean error remains higher at **3.8251**.
## Ineffective Approaches
- **Small geometry adjustments degrade aggregate performance.** Generations 26 and 28 both score **0.205291**, with mean error **3.8711**, **6.23% above Generation 22**. Their bounded **1.2109375–1.2265625** multipliers provide no aggregate advantage over the constant midpoint, extending Generation 21’s unsuccessful diameter adaptation.
- **Displacement adaptation still does not beat the best constant.** Generation 27, `smooth_displacement_radius`, scores **0.207251**, with mean error **4.96% above Generation 22**. This continues Generation 24’s unfavorable mean-error result for displacement conditioning, despite Generation 27’s better tail diagnostics.
- **Three particles with a wider radius perform substantially worse.** Generation 25, `three_particle_radius_refinement`, scores **0.187699**, with mean error **4.3277**, **18.76% above Generation 22** and above the corrected baseline. This reinforces the unfavorable allocation-reduction results from Generations 19/23. Because count and radius both differ from Generation 22, the regression cannot be assigned exclusively to either change.
## Implementation Insights
- **The leading behavior is a constant action pair, not demonstrated state adaptation.** Generation 22 ignores recovery, diameter, displacement and remaining budget; swarm size only caps allocation. Its multiplier produces absolute radii **0.609375** and **1.828125**. Across tested constants **1.0, 1.1875, 1.21875, 1.25 and 1.5**, performance remains nonmonotonic.
- **Generations 26 and 28 implement the same geometry rule.** Inspection confirms equivalent clipping, normalization and allocation logic, with identical errors in all 16 cases. They represent repeated behavior, extending the earlier duplicate-result pattern in Generations 16/18/20.
- **Integer allocation and fraction encoding remain distinct.** Generation 22 executes four objective-evaluated relocations across all **3,371 responses**, without horizon truncation. Encoding fraction **0.7** represents actual allocation **4/5 = 80%**; Generation 25’s encoding **0.5** represents **3/5 = 60%**.
- **Shared simulator mechanisms cannot explain an evolved implementation advantage.** Memory reevaluation and retained velocities are fixed; unselected particles still receive ordinary PSO updates and evaluations. Generation 22 spends **15.28–16.08%** of evaluations on detection and **0.32–1.93%** on memory refresh. Fewer relocations do not automatically save those evaluations.
