# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns
- **G11, Spread-Adaptive Partial Relocation, remains best:** score **0.214212**, mean offline error **3.6683**. Its three-particle allocation when normalized diameter exceeds four, otherwise four, with radius multiplier **1.25**, reduces mean error **2.40%** versus fixed-four G9 and **11.81%** versus corrected baseline G0 on the search suite.
- **G20’s threshold-five variant is the strongest new program:** score **0.213874**, mean error **3.6756**, placing it between G11 and G18 (**0.213608 / 3.6815**). It matches G11’s errors in **12 of 16 cases**, improves two and worsens two; the threshold change provides no aggregate improvement.
- **Predominantly four-particle relocation with occasional reductions remains the leading observed pattern.** G11 and G20 outperform fixed-four G9 (**0.210153**), fixed-three G14 (**0.199622**) and full-swarm G5 (**0.199277**) at multiplier **1.25**. These comparisons support the tested policy combinations, without isolating a causal benefit from current-state conditioning.
## Ineffective Approaches
- **Increasing G11’s compact-swarm radius worsens performance.** G22 preserves its allocation threshold but raises the four-particle branch multiplier from **1.25 to 1.3125**. Score falls to **0.209616**, mean error rises **2.79%** to **3.7706**, and 13 of 16 cases worsen. Together with G19’s unsuccessful reduction to **1.125** (**0.197054 / 4.0748**), both tested directions underperform the unchanged radius.
- **G21’s fitness-deterioration switch trails diameter conditioning.** Allocating three particles at relative deterioration ≤5%, otherwise four, produces **0.196264 / 4.0952**: **11.64% higher mean error than G11**, with worst-case error **12.3626**. This extends G10’s earlier negative result for a fitness-loss switch; successful validation does not establish useful allocation decisions.
- **Raising the diameter threshold does not improve G11.** G20’s threshold five increases mean error **0.20%**; G23/G24’s threshold six increases it **2.54%**, yielding **0.210023 / 3.7614**, slightly worse than fixed-four G9. Earlier threshold-eight G12 performs better than threshold six, so threshold changes show no monotonic performance trend.
## Implementation Insights
- **G11’s leading implementation is simple and stateless:** compute `swarm_diameter / max(default_radius, 1e-12)`, apply strict `> 4`, clamp the count to swarm size, and return multiplier **1.25**. Four-particle responses comprise **2,951/3,440 (85.78%)**. Its fixed multiplier still produces different absolute radii: **0.625** and **1.875** in the two severity regimes.
- **Similar allocation frequencies conceal different outcomes.** Three-particle responses represent **14.22%** for G11, **14.21%** for G20 and **14.11%** for G23/G24, despite their differing errors. Aggregate allocation frequency alone therefore does not explain the ranking; the policies also produce different response sequences and trajectories.
- **G23 and G24 duplicate the same tested behavior.** Comparing diameter against six times the guarded radius and dividing by that radius before comparing against six produce identical saved case metrics. Their matching results are not two distinct algorithmic improvements.
- **Adapter behavior remains a shared control.** Counts three and four correspond to actual allocation fractions **0.6/0.8**, while **0.5/0.7** only encode those integers. Memory reevaluation and retained velocities are not evolved gains. Requested, allocated and queried counts match without horizon truncation; non-relocated particles continue ordinary PSO motion.
## Performance Analysis
- **The leading order is now G11 > G20 > G18 > G12 > G9.** G20 misses G11 by only **0.00737 mean error**, whereas G23/G24, G22 and G21 miss it by **0.09312**, **0.10235** and **0.42690**, respectively. Rounded scores of **0.20–0.21** obscure these differences.
- **Case concentration remains central to interpreting G11’s advantage.** Against G9, G11 improves seven cases and worsens nine; its case_015 reduction exceeds its total paired reduction across all 16 cases. Excluding that case, G11 averages **3.2846**, versus G9’s **3.1990**. G20 changes only four case errors relative to G11 and leaves case_015 unchanged.
- **Mean and tail performance remain distinct.** G11, G20 and G23/G24 share worst-case error **9.4230**, but dispersion rises from **2.0817** to **2.0949** and **2.1294**. G22 raises worst-case error to **10.3487**. Earlier G14 retains a lower worst-case error of **9.0543**, despite substantially worse mean performance; the selection score rewards mean error alone.
- **Correctness and persistent tracking difficulty coexist.** All five new programs passed 16 cases. Case_015 interval-end error remains **7.2366** for G11, G20 and G23/G24, versus **7.9495** for G22 and **10.7897** for G21. Detection consumes roughly **16%** of evaluations, but these diagnostics do not establish the cause of performance differences or generalization beyond the reused search suite.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Shrink the radius only for dispersed swarms.** Preserve G11’s guarded normalization, strict `q > 4` allocation rule and count clamp, but use `radius_scale=1.125` or `1.0` in the three-particle branch as separate mutations; retain `1.25` otherwise. Fixed-three relocation performed better at radius `1.0` than `1.25`, making this a targeted interaction test that preserves G11’s dominant four-particle response.
2. **Test the remaining narrow threshold interval.** Keep radius `1.25` and implement `count = 3 if q > 4.5 else 4`, preserving the clamp. G20’s threshold five nearly matches G11, whereas six performs worse, so testing the midpoint between four and five is better supported than continuing to raise the threshold.
3. **Reduce G18’s displacement-dependent threshold adjustment.** Define `d = max(0.0, observed_best_displacement) / max(default_radius, 1e-12)` and use `T = 4 + a*d/(1+d)`, testing `a=0.5` and `a=1.0` separately, with three particles when `q>T` and radius fixed at `1.25`. G18’s existing amplitude of two remains competitive; smaller amplitudes test whether a weaker adjustment can improve on it while staying closer to G11.
4. **Probe a finer constant-radius neighborhood.** Preserve G11’s exact allocation rule and test constant multipliers `1.21875` and `1.28125` as separate candidates. The compact-branch failures at `1.125` and `1.3125` favor smaller perturbations around `1.25`, although they do not establish where the optimum lies when both branches change together.
5. **Try an observation-independent allocation mixture.** At radius `1.25`, select three particles with probability `0.1422` and four otherwise, retaining the count clamp and using a dedicated RNG initialized once per candidate execution, independent of simulator RNGs. This reproduces G11’s observed allocation proportion in expectation; its advantage over fixed-four is concentrated in one case, so the mixture tests whether occasional reductions remain useful without the spread trigger, without assuming equivalent trajectories or performance.
